SCR-103 · PRINCIPLE 1
top
Learn
Principle 1 of 4
?
Discipline is teaching
When a child misbehaves it's natural to ask how to stop it right now. But stopping a behavior and teaching a child what to do instead are two different things. Focus only on ending the moment and you miss the chance to build something they can use next time.
The old frame
"How do I stop this behavior right now?"
Instead
"What skill is my child missing, and how do I teach it?"
Key concept
Behavior management stops discomfort in the moment. Teaching builds a skill for next time.

top

Principle 2 of 4
?
Look beneath the behavior
When your child melts down, screams or refuses, the behavior is what you see — but it isn't the whole story. Instead of assuming they're choosing to be difficult, look underneath. What are they trying to tell you that they can't yet put into words?
The old frame
"She's choosing to be difficult and push my buttons."
Key concept
Behavior is the symptom on the surface. Missing skills and distress are the causes underneath.
ICEBERG
Behavior above the waterline, unmet need below. Interactive — tap a layer.

SCR-105 · PRINCIPLE 3 · BRAIN-STATE TOGGLE
top
Learn
Principle 3 of 4
?
State comes before skill
When a child is overwhelmed their brain switches to survival mode and logic is genuinely out of reach. Before you can teach anything or hold a limit, you both have to get back to calm.
The child's state
Your state
BRAIN STATE
Survival state, locked · Calm state, open to learning
Distress locks the thinking part of the brain. You can't teach a skill into an active storm — and if you're angry too, your state adds to theirs.

SCR-106 · PRINCIPLE 4 · TWO-STEP FORMULA
top
Learn
Principle 4 of 4
?
Connection before correction
Parents often worry that validating frustration means giving up control. It doesn't. Connection isn't permission — it's the bridge that lets your limit be heard. Acknowledge how they feel first and the boundary lands instead of bouncing.
The old frame
"If I validate her feelings, I'm giving in and letting her win."
TWO-STEP FORMULA
Acknowledge the feeling, then state the limit — with worked examples.
"You wanted to stay at the park. It's time to go home now." Understanding is not permission.

about this module
About
What this is, and what it isn't
An independent learning project, built to see whether a parent could practice these conversations rather than just read about them. Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or reviewed by Conscious Discipline or Loving Guidance. The approach is theirs; the interpretation, the wording and any mistakes are mine.
WHAT HAPPENS TO WHAT YOU TYPED
It stays on this device, in this browser. Nothing is sent anywhere, there is no account, and no one reads it — including me.
HOW THE FEEDBACK WORKS
Your answers are matched against written criteria — rules, on your own device. Not a person, not a judgment, and sometimes wrong about a good answer.
IF YOU ARE WORRIED ABOUT YOUR CHILD
This module is not advice, assessment, or a substitute for a conversation with your child's teacher, doctor or a professional. If something feels more serious than a hard morning, it probably is — talk to someone.

READING THIS FOR COPY
Three things worth watching for on a straight read, because they are the ones a flow view exposes and a single-screen view hides:

Repetition across screens. Each screen was written to stand alone, so reassurances about privacy, saving and not being graded may now land three times in a row.

Voice drift. Section 1 was written before Section 3. The later screens are warmer and shorter; the principle screens may read as the textbook by comparison.

Promises made early and kept late. SCR-101 sets an expectation about what the module will do; CLOSE-2 is where it either lands or doesn't.

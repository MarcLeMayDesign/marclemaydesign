Why Instructional Design? Why now?

I spent more than two decades as a designer and educator in higher ed. I loved teaching: the interactions with students, the thrill of seeing them grow, and the fun and challenge of designing effective learning.

But I’ve encountered two main obstacles to continuing my career in academia:

  1. I resigned a full-time faculty position at Georgia State University to support my wife’s academic career at FSU and be the primary caretaker at home. I’d make the same choice again, but both of us building academic careers in one college town hasn’t been sustainable.
  2. The pace and evaluative frameworks of higher ed left me frustrated. My attempts to innovate curricula to make them more learner-centric, technology-forward, and culturally relevant have met a lot of resistance from an entrenched system. And with a culture short on accountability, change is slow and problems remain unfixed.

What I Want Now

To put my experience and skills to work in a faster-paced environment that is strong on critique and accountability, all with the goal of shipping the best learning experiences possible in the time they’re needed. That is why I am pivoting to full-time instructional design.
